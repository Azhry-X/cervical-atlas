1 · JOSPT CPG & Manipulation vs Mobilisation BLANPIED 2017 · GROSS 2015
JOSPT CPG — mobility deficits Blanpied 2017 (Guideline): acute: “should provide thoracic manipulation, a program of neck ROM exercises, and scapulothoracic and upper extremity strengthening”; “may provide cervical manipulation and/or mobilization”.
JOSPT CPG — chronic headache (Blanpied 2017 JOSPT, Guideline): “cervical or cervicothoracic manipulation or mobilizations combined with shoulder girdle and neck stretching, strengthening, and endurance exercise”.
Manipulation vs mobilisation (Cochrane) (Gross 2015 Cochrane) M: similar results for every outcome; because of rare but serious adverse events, research should focus on mobilisation.
2 · Thoracic Manipulation Evidence MASARACCHIO 2019 · CROSS 2011
Thoracic manipulation (MA) (Masaracchio 2019 PLoS One) M: vs mobilisation: pain MD −13.63 (−21.79 to −5.46), disability −9.93 (−14.38 to −5.48); vs standard care: pain −13.21, disability −11.36 short term; vs cervical manipulation: no difference.
Thoracic manipulation (SR) (Cross 2011 JOSPT) L: short-term improvement in acute/subacute mechanical neck pain; literature weak.
3 · Cervicogenic Headache Trials (C1–C2 Self-SNAG & Jull 2002) HALL 2007 · JULL 2002
C1–C2 self-SNAG (cervicogenic headache RCT) (Hall 2007 JOSPT) M: FRT range +15° vs +5° placebo; headache index 31 vs 51 at 4 weeks, 24 vs 44 at 12 months; overall reduction 54%.
Manipulation + exercise (cervicogenic headache RCT) (Jull 2002 Spine) H: both reduced headache frequency and intensity, maintained at 12 months; combined not significantly superior (10% more relieved).
4 · Dry Needling vs Physical Modalities CHEN 2025 MA
Dry needling (MA vs physical modalities) (Chen 2025 J Bodyw Mov Ther) L: no difference in pain (VAS MD −0.13 cm) or NDI.

5 · Adverse Events, Vascular Safety Screen & IFOMPT Framework CARNES 2010 · SWAIT & FINCH 2017 · IFOMPT
Adverse events (SR) (Carnes 2010 Man Ther) M: minor/moderate transient events ≈ 41% (17–68%) in cohorts, 22% (11.1–36.2%) in RCTs; major ≈ 0.13%.
Serious adverse events (scoping) (Swait & Finch 2017 Chiropr Man Therap) L: 1 per 2 million manipulations to 13 per 10,000 patients; association (causality unclear) with cervical arterial stroke under 45 years.
Cervical artery dysfunction — IFOMPT International Framework (2023) Rushton 2023 (Guideline): vascular pathologies of the neck are rare but must be considered; recognised mainly through history-taking and adapted physical examination; positional VBI testing and craniovertebral ligament testing are excluded from the IFOMPT Framework (Hutting 2022) M.
6 · Dry Needling Safety & Anatomical Danger Zones SAFETY SCREEN
Adverse event rates: Brady 2014 M: mild AEs 19.18 %; upper risk rate for significant AEs ≤ 0.04 %; bruising 7.55 %, bleeding 4.65 % • Boyce 2020 M: minor AE 36.7 %; major AE < .1 % (prolonged symptom aggravation .03 %, fainting .02 %, forgotten needles .01 %; major AEs most often with gluteal, lumbar paraspinal, suboccipital and upper trapezius needling)
Serious risks & screening: Halle 2016 E: pneumothorax, cardiac tamponade, haematoma, infection, CNS injury; filament needles 32–36 gauge (.25–.20 mm) • Kearns 2019 E: anticoagulants / coagulopathy — caution — possible increased incidence or severity of haematoma and bruising
Cervical / scapular danger zones: Kearns 2022 L: upper trapezius — a 30 mm needle never reached the pleural space; consider needles < 40 mm • Valera-Calero 2021 M: rhomboid major — insert a maximum of 19 mm to reach the deep limit of rhomboid major; sex, BMI and thorax circumference explain 69.7 % of pleura depth • Okçu 2026 L: parascapular region — women: shorter skin-to-pleura distance; depth increases with BMI
