CPG Classification, Clinical Clusters & Prognostic Trajectories

Patient
02 / 09
CPG classification & prognostic stratification: classify into one of the four JOSPT 2017 CPG categories (Blanpied 2017, Guideline), screen radiculopathy (Wainner 2003 / ULTT A) and cervicogenic headache (FRT), and document baseline prognostic factors.
1 · JOSPT CPG Classification: Mobility & Coordination Blanpied 2017
Neck pain with mobility deficits (Blanpied 2017 JOSPT, Guideline): central and/or unilateral neck symptoms with restricted active cervical ROM, with neck pain reproduced at end range and on cervical / upper thoracic segmental mobility testing (p3/p7).
Neck pain with movement coordination impairments (including WAD) (Blanpied 2017 JOSPT, Guideline): whiplash-associated or insidious neck pain with impaired deep cervical flexor activation (CCFT), DNF endurance, or sensorimotor control (JPE) (p3/p8).
Whiplash (WAD) grading (Spitzer 1995 Spine) E: Quebec Task Force grades 0–IV (definitions on p1).
2 · JOSPT CPG Classification: Headache & Radiating PainBLANPIED 2017 CPG
Neck pain with headaches (cervicogenic) (Blanpied 2017 JOSPT, Guideline): unilateral/cervically triggered headache aggravated by neck movement or sustained posture, with positive flexion-rotation test and upper cervical mobility restriction (p3/p7).
Neck pain with radiating pain (radicular) (Blanpied 2017 JOSPT, Guideline): neck + upper-extremity radicular pain/paresthesia evaluated with ULTT A, Spurling, distraction, and Valsalva (p3/p4/p6).
3 · Cervical Radiculopathy Cluster (Wainner 2003) WAINNER 2003 SPINE
Wainner radiculopathy cluster (Wainner 2003 Spine) M: 4-item cluster LR+ 30.3 (wide CI); ULTT A most useful to rule out (items: see original paper).
Rule-out screen (Wainner 2003 / Rubinstein 2007) M: ULTT A Sn 0.97 (0.90–1.0), Sp 0.22 (0.12–0.33) → negative rules out (see p4 for single-test values).
Radiculopathy Screen: ULTT A (−) rules outWainner cluster (+) LR+ 30.3

Flexion-rotation test (FRT) — Hall (Hall 2008 J Manipulative Physiol Ther) M: Sn 90%, Sp 88%, κ 0.85, usable by inexperienced examiners.

5 · Clinical Course, Whiplash Predictors & Radiculopathy Prognosis HUSH · WALTON · RITCHIE · WONG
Acute neck pain prognosis (MA) (Hush 2011 Arch Phys Med Rehabil) H: pain 64 (61–67) at onset → 35 (32–38) at 6.5 weeks → 42 (39–45) at 12 months; disability 30 → 17 by 6.5 weeks, no further improvement at 12 months.
Whiplash poor-outcome predictors (MA) (Walton 2013 JOSPT) M: high baseline pain (> 5.5/10), NDI > 14.5/50, headache at inception, female sex, less than post-secondary education, WAD grade 2 or 3 (robust to publication bias).
Whiplash course + WhipPredict CPR (Ritchie 2013 Pain) L: ≈ 50% fully recover, 25% persistent moderate/severe pain and disability, 25% milder disability; WhipPredict CPR PPV 71% (older age + high NDI + hyperarousal → chronic; younger + low NDI → recovery).
Radiculopathy course (Wong 2014 Spine J) M: substantial improvement within 4–6 months; complete recovery in ≈ 83% by 24–36 months; workers’ compensation = poorer prognosis.
WAD recovery advice (CPG) (Blanpied 2017 JOSPT, Guideline): “Reassurance to the patient that recovery is expected to occur within the first 2 to 3 months”.

Dysphagia after ACDF (Zero-P cohort, n = 260) (Xue 2022 Orthop Surg) L: risk factors: more operated levels, longer operation time, larger dC2–7 angle change and dPSTT; of dysphagia cases 37.1% resolved by 3 months, 38.6% by 6 months, 17.1% by 12 months.

2 · ACDF + Physiotherapy vs Structured Physiotherapy Alone ENGQUIST 2013 / 2017 · PEOLSSON 2013
ACDF + physio vs physio alone (radiculopathy, 2 y) (Engquist 2013 Spine) H: more rapid improvement in first year; “better/much better” 87% vs 62% at 12 months (P < 0.05), 81% vs 69% at 24 months (P = 0.28); NDI not different (P = 0.23); both groups improved.
Same trial, physical function (Peolsson 2013 Spine) H: no difference in neck ROM, neck muscle endurance or hand function; “a structured physiotherapy program should precede a decision for ACDF”.
Same trial, 5–8 years (Engquist 2017 J Neurosurg Spine) H: NDI reduced 21% (surgery) vs 11% (physio), p = 0.03; neck pain −39 mm vs −19 mm; arm pain no significant difference; “better/much better” 93% vs 62%.
3 · Structured Post-Op Physiotherapy After ACDF WIBAULT 2018
Structured post-op physiotherapy after ACDF (RCT) (Wibault 2018 J Neurosurg Spine) M: only minor additional benefit at 6 months (expectation fulfilment); 61% of standard-care patients sought extra physiotherapy.
ACDF — expected outcome (Peolsson 2007 Eur Spine J) M: approximate 50 % probability of pain relief and little probability of functional improvement.

Collar or physio vs wait-and-see (recent radiculopathy) (Kuijper 2009 BMJ) H: collar + rest or 6 weeks physio + home exercise: extra arm-pain reduction 12 mm vs wait-and-see at 6 weeks; neck pain −17 mm (collar), −14 mm (physio).

Traction + PT (MA) (Romeo 2018 Phys Ther) M: mechanical traction: pain g −0.85 (−1.63 to −0.06) short term, −1.17 (−2.25 to −0.10) intermediate; disability g −1.05 (−1.81 to −0.28) intermediate.
2 · Low-Level Laser & Electrotherapy CHOW 2009 · KROELING 2013
Low-level laser (MA, 16 RCTs, 820 pts) (Chow 2009 Lancet) M: acute: RR 1.69 (1.22–2.33); chronic: RR 4.05 (2.74–5.98); VAS −19.86 mm (10.04–29.68); follow-up 1–22 weeks −22.07 mm (17.42–26.72).

5 · Adverse Events, Vascular Safety Screen & IFOMPT Framework CARNES 2010 · SWAIT & FINCH 2017 · IFOMPT
Adverse events (SR) (Carnes 2010 Man Ther) M: minor/moderate transient events ≈ 41% (17–68%) in cohorts, 22% (11.1–36.2%) in RCTs; major ≈ 0.13%.

1 · Exercise for Neck Pain & Headache GROSS 2015 COCHRANE · JULL 2002
Exercise for neck pain (Cochrane, 27 trials) (Gross 2015 Cochrane) M: acute: no evidence; chronic: cervico-scapulothoracic + upper-limb strengthening — pain SMD −0.71 (−1.33 to −0.10); combined strengthening + stretching — function SMD −0.45 (−0.72 to −0.18).
Manipulation + exercise (cervicogenic headache RCT) (Jull 2002 Spine) H: both reduced headache frequency and intensity, maintained at 12 months; combined not significantly superior (10% more relieved).
2 · Whiplash Trials (MINT & PROMISE) LAMB 2013 · MICHALEFF 2014
MINT (acute whiplash, n = 3,851 + 599) (Lamb 2013 Lancet) H: active ED management: NDI difference 0.5 (−1.5 to 2.5) at 12 months — no benefit; physio package vs single advice: NDI −3.7 (−6.1 to −1.3) at 4 months only; not cost-effective.
PROMISE (chronic whiplash, n = 172) (Michaleff 2014 Lancet) H: 12-week exercise programme = advice: pain difference 0.0 (−0.7 to 0.7) at 14 weeks, −0.1 (−0.8 to 0.6) at 12 months.

WAD acute (CPG) Blanpied 2017 (Guideline): educate to return to normal activities as soon as possible, minimise collar use, postural and mobility exercises, reassure “recovery is expected to occur within the first 2 to 3 months”.
