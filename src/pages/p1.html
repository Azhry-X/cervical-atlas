B · Trauma Clearance (CCR / NEXUS), Myelopathy, CAD & Upper Cervical Instability TriageTRIAGE GATE
Trauma Imaging Rules & Serious Pathology ScreenJOSPT 2017
Canadian C-Spine Rule (Stiell 2001 JAMA) H: 3 questions: (1) high-risk (age ≥ 65 years, dangerous mechanism, extremity paresthesias) → image; (2) low-risk (simple rear-end MVC, sitting in ED, ambulatory, delayed neck pain, no midline tenderness) → ROM check; (3) active rotation 45° L & R → no imaging. Sn 100% (98–100%), Sp 42.5% (40–44%)
CCR vs NEXUS (Stiell 2003 NEJM) H: Sn 99.4% vs 90.7%; Sp 45.1% vs 36.8%; radiography rates 55.9% vs 66.6% (all P < 0.001) · NEXUS (Hoffman 2000 NEJM) H: Sn 99.0% (98.0–99.6%); Sp 12.9%; NPV 99.8%
Serious pathology (CPG) (Blanpied 2017 JOSPT) (Guideline): “Clinicians should perform assessments … to determine the potential for the presence of serious pathology (eg, infection, cancer, cardiac involvement, arterial insufficiency, upper cervical ligamentous insufficiency, unexplained cranial nerve dysfunction or fracture), and refer for consultation as indicated.”
Cervical Artery Dysfunction & Positional VBI TestingIFOMPT
Cervical artery dysfunction (Rushton 2023 JOSPT) (Guideline): IFOMPT Framework: vascular pathologies of the neck are rare but must be considered; recognised mainly through history-taking and adapted physical examination
Positional VBI tests (Hutting 2013 · Hutting 2022) M: Sn 0–57%, Sp 67–100%; “the premanipulative tests do not seem valid”; positional and craniovertebral ligament testing excluded from IFOMPT · CAD symptoms: unaccustomed headache/neck pain, diplopia, dysarthria, dysphagia, drop attacks, dizziness, facial paraesthesia, ataxia, cranial nerve deficits E
Cervical Myelopathy Cluster & Upper Motor Neuron ScreenCook 2010
Cervical myelopathy cluster (Cook) (Cook 2010 J Man Manip Ther) M: 5 items: gait deviation, + Hoffmann, inverted supinator sign, + Babinski, age > 45 y. ≤ 1 of 5 positive: LR− 0.18 (0.12–0.42) → rules out; ≥ 3 of 5: LR+ 30.9 (5.5–181.8), post-test 94%; 4 of 5: post-test 99+% (pre-test 35%)
Myelopathy single tests (Cook 2010) M: inverted supinator sign LR+ 29.1 (5.1–171.5); Achilles hyperreflexia 7.8 (2.5–25.4); quadriceps hyperreflexia 6.9 (2.8–17.5); Hoffmann LR+ 4.9 (2.6–9.6); age > 45 y LR− 0.48 (0.26–0.85)
Hoffmann vs cord compression (Glaser 2001) L: Sn 58%, Sp 78% · Myelopathy tests (SR) (Cook 2011) M: nearly all tests specific but poorly sensitive → poor screening tools alone
Upper Cervical Instability & Quebec Task Force WAD GradesInstability & WAD
Sharp-Purser test (RA) (Uitvlugt 1988 Arthritis Rheum) L: Sp 96%; Sn 88% when subluxation > 4 mm
Upper cervical instability tests (SR) (Hutting 2013 Phys Ther) M: 5 studies; specificity sufficient, sensitivity variable; membrane tests best — no pooled numbers
Whiplash (WAD) grades (Spitzer 1995 Spine) E: Quebec Task Force grades 0–IV (0: no neck complaint/signs; I: pain/stiffness only; II: neck complaint + MSK signs; III: neck complaint + neurological signs; IV: fracture/dislocation)
